## Opening summary

Today's work turned the 2021 anomaly from a vague concern about a sensitive 2014-2024 slope into a tightly localized and testable phenomenon.

The robust facts at the end of the day are:

- the 2021 pulse exists in **raw** Annual VNL radiance, so it is not created by the project's 0.235 post-2017 correction;
- the 2022 reversal is strongly paired with the 2021 increase;
- the disturbance appears in both annual mean and annual median product layers;
- annual mask/support changes contribute substantially in dim regions, but the anomaly remains strongly on **persistent identical pixel support**;
- the 2017 calibration step behaves as an intensity-only positive control, whereas the 2021 event contains both support and intensity components;
- the July VCM/VCMSL stray-light pilot does not explain the northern annual pulse;
- full monthly VCMSLCFG localization shows that the annual anomaly is overwhelmingly concentrated in **January and February 2021**, with February especially strong and strongly latitude-structured;
- this timing makes winter surface state, particularly snow/albedo, a serious mechanism to test using the published Levin/Levin & Zhang analytical framework.

A first snow analysis appeared extremely strong, but a physical-range invariant exposed a preprocessing error: MOD10CM categorical values >100 had leaked into zonal means. Those numerical snow results are therefore **invalid and must not be cited**. A raster-level fix has been committed. The next session starts by rerunning the snow stage with all categorical codes physically converted to nodata before extraction.

## 9. Snow/albedo hypothesis and published-method design

The January/February localization matches the mechanism described in:

- Noam Levin (2017), *The impact of seasonal changes on observed nighttime brightness from 2014 to 2015 monthly VIIRS DNB composites*, Remote Sensing of Environment 193:150-164. DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2017.03.003.
- Noam Levin & Qingling Zhang (2017), *A global analysis of factors controlling VIIRS nighttime light levels from densely populated areas*, Remote Sensing of Environment 190:366-382. DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2017.01.006.

Those papers motivated retaining both:

- mean VIIRS brightness;
- snow cover;
- cloud-free coverage;
- January-vs-July comparisons;
- percent-lit area at published thresholds: 2, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100 and 250 nW cm^-2 sr^-1;
- Spearman rank associations.

For the project-specific causal diagnostic, February was pre-specified as the **primary month**, January as supportive, and March as a negative control.

## 10. Snow analysis quality-control failure — preliminary result INVALID

The first complete snow run produced a superficially compelling result:

```text
February:
    regional rho ~ +0.661
    country-median rho ~ +0.735
    adjusted snow coefficient positive
    snow x baseline-brightness interaction highly significant

March:
    rho ~ -0.027
```

This matched the pre-specified decision rule almost perfectly.

However, inspection of the regional snow values showed some `snow_mean_pct` values greater than 100%, with some values approaching ~247%. That is physically impossible.

MOD10CM defines only 0-100 as snow percentage. Values such as 211, 250, 253, 254 and 255 are categorical codes for night/cloud/no-decision/water/fill. These codes had leaked through the custom Python masking layer when `exactextract` handled the raster source.

### Decision

**All numerical snow-analysis results from that run are invalid and must not be cited as evidence.**

This is an important quality-control success: the result looked unusually convincing, but the physical 0-100% invariant exposed a preprocessing error before it was accepted.

### Fix

Commit:

```text
2d79e1da  Mask MOD10CM categorical codes before snow extraction
```

The fix is raster-level:

- convert every MOD10CM value >100 to true raster nodata (255) in the cached GeoTIFF;
- version the snow-cleaning cache so old extractions cannot be silently reused;
- assert that every finite extracted `snow_mean_pct` lies in 0-100;
- abort immediately if the invariant fails.

### Current status

**No valid conclusion has yet been drawn about whether snow explains the 2021 anomaly.**

The hypothesis remains strong and well motivated, but the corrected rerun is required.

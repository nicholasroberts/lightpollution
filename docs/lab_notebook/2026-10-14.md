more artificial light is available to reflect.

## 5. Important limitation: no universal positive snow-radiance seasonality

Across all available paired months in 2020-2022:

```text
N regions with >=18 paired months = 1495
median within-region snow-radiance Spearman rho = -0.1751
fraction positive = 27.8%
```

### KEEP interpretation

Do not generalize the result to "more snow always makes European NUTS3 regions
brighter in VIIRS". The evidence is strongest for the specific winter 2021
disturbance and for the January-vs-July contrast in that year.

This difference from the strongest published urban seasonal examples is not a
reason to reject the February result: the present analysis covers a much broader
mixture of European NUTS3 landscapes and uses a different anomaly design.
However, it is an important limitation to preserve for the paper.

## 7. Rationale for the next analysis

The snow test strongly supports the winter-surface hypothesis but snow cover
itself is not the final optical mechanism. Snow changes the reflectance/albedo of
the illuminated surface.

The next stage should therefore add:

1. MODIS surface albedo, ideally a broadband visible BRDF/albedo product on a CMG
   grid, retaining both white-sky and black-sky albedo;
2. monthly NDVI as the complementary vegetation/surface-state variable used in
   the published Levin analyses.

The same anomaly and control structure should be retained:

```text
February primary
January supportive
March negative control

surface anomaly = value_2021 - mean(value_2020, value_2022)

controls:
    country
    latitude
    baseline VIIRS brightness
    cf_cvg anomaly

interaction:
    surface-state anomaly x baseline brightness
```

The key mechanistic question is whether adding albedo attenuates the snow term
while albedo itself strongly predicts the VIIRS anomaly. This should be treated
as a mechanistic attenuation test rather than formal causal mediation.

## 1. Scientific objective and principles reaffirmed

The aim is **forward prediction**, not merely a historical correlation between log population density and log VIIRS radiance. First predict physically meaningful future regional radiance; later transfer those radiance predictions through the independently calibrated relationship to the visibility of the nocturnal sky-polarisation cue. Biological relevance requires preserving physical radiance changes, not treating a 50% increase from a nearly dark baseline as equivalent to a 50% increase in a brightly lit region.

Key methodological requirements:

1. Keep the **same** NUTS2016 polygons (no recoding across 2020/2021/2023), 1,514 NUTS3 regions, and density groups derived from each region's **2014–2020 median Eurostat density**. Exclude 2013 because of its previously demonstrated leverage.
2. Model **all 11 annual observations** for each region. Report observed year-on-year differences, fitted trajectories, absolute units, relative (%) units, and future-year holdouts. Endpoint maps/boxplots are an important *independent descriptive check*.
3. Use the globally applied production 2017+ calibration offset and the specifically validated but **imperfect** 2021 surface-reflectance correction. Do not confuse original, pixel-calibrated and fully corrected columns.
4. Prefer **mechanistically interpretable** settlement/demographic predictors, such as population density and ultimately population-density change. A smaller p value or in-sample BIC by itself is not a biological/physical justification.
5. Country intercepts can control historical baseline level; extra country-specific *time slopes* require a separate justification and credible future transferability.
6. Evaluate proportional errors **and** errors in **nW cm^-2 sr^-1**, with MAE/robust errors and transparent treatment of unusually bright regions; a few extreme measurements can dominate nW RMSE.
7. Be explicit about the distinction between in-sample historical reconstruction and prospective forecast tests. The originally chosen short one-year RMSE threshold was not aligned with the decade-scale scientific goal.
8. Do not delete an influential region merely because it produces a large residual. First determine *when* its radiance changed, if adjacent regions share the change, and whether radiance measurement, agricultural/industrial illumination or spectral technology might be involved.

**Pixel calibration** applied to *every positive VIIRS pixel* beginning in 2017: \`max(raw_pixel - 0.235, 0)\` in nW cm^-2 sr^-1; zero/background pixels are not converted into negatives. Only **afterwards** are spherical-area-weighted exact zonal means calculated. It is **not** a flat subtraction from the regional mean. Earlier years are not offset. See [2017 calibration note](../investigations/viirs_2017_calibration.md).

**2021 surface correction:** a further empirical estimate of excess 2021 winter snow/albedo-related measured radiance, implemented as a log-ratio-derived adjustment to the already instrument-calibrated NUTS2016 annual value. The correction is applied to **2021 only**. Its derivation/limits are documented in \`src/build_fixed_nuts2016_2021_surface_correction.py\`, \`surface_correction_summary.txt\` and the [2021 investigation](../investigations/viirs_2021_anomaly.md). The density class **30–100 people/km²** retains a concern about incomplete/atypical correction; do **not** claim the anomaly has been entirely removed.

Because only 2021 is additionally modified, observed **2014 and 2024 endpoints are identical** in the pre-snow and fully corrected series; this does *not* mean that a longitudinal model can omit the 2021 correction.

### 7.1 Models to fit, and meaning of centred time

Two annual mixed-effects models, with NUTS3 random intercepts and country intercepts:

\`\`\`text
M0: log10(R_it) ~ year_c + fixed density band + country + (1 | NUTS3)
M1: log10(R_it) ~ year_c * fixed density band + country + (1 | NUTS3)
year_c = calendar_year - 2019
\`\`\`

**2019 is purely the mathematical centre** of the 2014–2024 interval: \`year_c\` ranges from -5 through +5. It is **not** a breakpoint, a special intervention year, a preference for 2019 data, or an assumption that the trend changes in 2019. Centering changes intercept interpretation and numerical stability, **not** the fitted linear slope or the 2014→2024 difference. The 2021 correction is applied to observed radiance **before fitting**, not as a time hinge.

The model estimates continuous density-class-specific **annual** trends, though the functional form assumes they are linear on the log-radiance scale; this assumption must be judged against the full observed trajectory, particularly the 2021 point.

## 8. Why the earlier H/country analyses do NOT determine the final model

Three separate conclusions must not be conflated:

- **Descriptive/in-sample:** five density groups diverge and H terms can have very small formal p values. Country-specific slopes also fit country mean trajectories much better historically.
- **Predictive:** H improves physical radiance RMSE only about 1% in the pre-snow comparison, and regional H composition accounts for only about 3% of the country residual trend. Country-slope terms change the percentage-error metric more than the absolute one and do not uniformly improve country-specific predictions. The pre-snow absolute pooled RMSE is additionally distorted by NL333.
- **Scientific mechanism:** topography constrains settlement but is nearly time-invariant. Differences in how greenhouse illumination, lighting technology, economic activity and policy change over time cannot be derived from H alone. Independent evidence for demographic growth \`ΔD\` has not yet been validated on the *same fixed 2014–2024 NUTS2016 cohort*.

Therefore **do not automatically reintroduce H or country-specific time slopes** in the next primary model. Preserve all those comparative outputs for supplementary discussion of excluded predictors.

## Evening research pivot — historical biological polarization-cue visibility (16 October)

**Decision:** The next main study should reconstruct historical changes in celestial polarization-receptor contrast (PRC) across Europe during 2014–2024 at local VIIRS spatial resolution (roughly 500 m to 1 km), rather than start with speculative SSP-based future radiance projections. The forward prediction project remains valuable, but becomes a later stage. The earlier NUTS2016 radiance/density work stays intact as provenance and supplementary material.

The biological response derived in Figure 3D of the team's recent paper is nonlinear. Large reductions in upward satellite radiance need not produce large gains in animal receptor contrast when the site remains intensely illuminated. Smaller changes in relatively dark habitats could be more consequential. Therefore map a local PRC response first, and only then aggregate habitat area, NUTS3 and national results. Do not equate continuous receptor contrast with established navigational detectability in the absence of species-specific behavioural thresholds. Investigate the transfer function in further taxa and geographical conditions without assuming its coefficients are universal.

### Critical original site-predictor provenance — confirmed by the investigator

**The published experimental site predictor came from Light Pollution Map's VIIRS 2019 radiance layer, identified by the encoded overlay value `viirs_2019`. It did not come from the site's modelled sky-brightness layer.** The earlier example link carrying `sb_2025` reflected a different map view and must not be used as evidence for the original extraction.

The 2019 VIIRS overlay is our calibration reference. The exact historical data supplier/product version, compositing method, pixel sampling method and website processing date must still be established, since the present website may use a different backend from the one used during manuscript preparation. The map coordinates in the shared URL set the **display centre**, not the individual study sites.

### First test, before any continent-wide reconstruction

1. Obtain the original site IDs, geographic coordinates, field measurement dates, PRC measurements, species identity, site-specific numeric 2019 VIIRS values, uncertainty estimates and original best-fit functions/code. Preserve published values and reproduce the existing curve before any re-fitting.
2. Independently sample **actual 2019 EOG Annual VNL pixels** at the identical coordinates. Report raw 2019 values and our 2017+ instrument-corrected values separately: neither may be assumed identical to the website popup.
3. Independently sample **NASA Black Marble VNP46A4 annual 2019 snow-free radiance**, retaining product identification, flags, counts, pixel coverage and observation geometry. These are two different synthesis methods applied to related VIIRS observations, not independent satellite radiometers.
4. Compare containing-pixel values with **predeclared** nearby spatial supports (roughly 500 m/1 km and physically motivated larger neighbourhoods), accounting for coordinate uncertainty, pixel grids, acquisition/field-year mismatch and the fact that skyglow can arise from remote sources. Do not optimise search radius merely to maximise an empirical correlation.
5. Validate published PRC predictions against the newly extracted site radiances. Distinguish refitting accuracy from geographically held-out prediction, and evaluate fitted-form uncertainty, species parameters, outliers, atmospheric and spectral assumptions.
6. Only after that test, plan annual Europe-wide pixel-scale mapping using the full 2014–2024 record, with all years (not just endpoint changes), quality checks and a Black Marble processing sensitivity. The previous special **2021 snow correction was estimated on NUTS3 annual aggregates and must not be blindly applied to individual pixels**.

Other uncertainties: VIIRS underweights blue (~450 nm) emissions from many LEDs; skyglow and polarization patterns depend on emission geometry and atmospheric conditions as well as local upward radiance; the timing of a satellite annual composite need not match a field experiment. Explore whether an extended multi-species mechanistic PRC function is justifiable.

## Working PRC transfer function adopted — 19 October 2026

The investigator has expressly accepted, **for now**, the new exponential PRC relationship fitted to the project's **calibrated EOG VIIRS annual radiance in the field measurement year** as the *working function* for the next stages of historical PRC mapping. This supersedes the earlier intention to use the original website-based fit as the default. The original website fit remains a sensitivity comparator.

The provisional function is

```text
PRC(R) = 0.144845 + (0.461175 - 0.144845) * exp(-0.106483 * R)
       = 0.144845 + 0.316330 * exp(-0.106483 * R)
```

with corrected EOG radiance `R` in nW cm^-2 sr^-1. Fitted from the 16 provisionally site-matched observations drawn from the original 18-entry PRC vector, involving 14 Table S1 sites. Fitted goodness of fit `R²=0.949279`, `RMSE=0.027146`; site-blocked hold-out `RMSE=0.032101`. The fitted parameter triplet is rounded to six decimals for this frozen working definition; the fitting code retains full precision during fitting.

**Explicit provisional limits:** the R original measurement filenames are not yet recovered, four assignments are not unique, two original plotted observations have no Table S1 location, and a good in-sample fit does not validate behavioural visibility thresholds or cross-species/general-geography transport. No new European PRC maps have been computed at this decision point.

**February cases at -0.235 offset:** `LV006 Rīga` spatial PRC 0.2784 versus uniform 0.2147 (+0.0637); `PL633 Trójmiejski` 0.2809 versus 0.2168 (+0.0641); `UKM84 North Lanarkshire` 0.3377 versus 0.2826 (+0.0550); `PL911 Miasto Warszawa` 0.2281 versus 0.1784 (+0.0497); `NL125 Zuidwest-Friesland` 0.3583 versus 0.3739 (−0.0156). The corresponding February raw 2021 mean VIIRS radiances were ~69.5, 66.2, 30.9, 74.5 and 1.7 nW cm^-2 sr^-1 respectively. Strong spatial effects are concentrated in high-brightness / high-anomaly regions; extrapolation to dark places remains uncertain.

**Important model admissibility warnings:** the predicted surface correction is often extremely large relative to the local observed monthly VIIRS signal. February equal-mean multiplier `q` ranges from **0.288 to 2.419** across the 18 pilot regions; `q>1` in 6/18 (i.e. the attributed surface signal is negative and correction increases radiance), and `q<0.5` in one. January `q` ranges **0.227–4.054**, and March `q` reaches **5.657** despite being the nominal negative-control month. The pixelwise spatial correction clips originally positive-or-zero VIIRS radiance to zero in a median **22.9% of the analysed pixels within each February region**, with a maximum **69.3%** in a region (unweighted regional medians); these strong truncations could drive nonlinear PRC differences rather than a trustworthy physical snow estimate. In March, Rīga still has a spatial-uniform difference ~0.0225 PRC, not negligible despite little overall March radiance RMSE improvement.

**Coverage caveat:** `predicted_pixel_fraction_common` has a median of 1.000 but is conditional on the subset of VIIRS pixels with complete 2020/21/22 snow/radiance support; it does not guarantee all pixels inside each NUTS region are represented. January DEB17 `predicted_pixel_fraction_common` is ~0.494. Restriction to snow-affected 18 selected regions is not a Europe-representative sampling design.

**Scientific decision:** spatial allocation demonstrably matters *conditional on the current predicted surface effects*, so the equality-of-mean-radiance assumption alone does not justify a uniform regional PRC correction. But the unconstrained current spatial model also shows substantial extrapolation/truncation artefacts, so the result does **not** support adopting it as a corrected pixel field. The annual post-2016 `−0.235` shift remains validated only in its annual EOG processing context; applying it to monthly outputs is an explicit sensitivity, not a settled calibration. No 2021 annual dataset or historical PRC atlas was overwritten.

**Next methodological step:** validate or constrain the physically plausible *surface-associated* effect by prior brightness and snow-state strata, rerun snow-free/control-year placebo diagnostics and inspect negative predicted surface components and clipping, before trying a monthly-to-annual weighted bridge. Compare region-uniform approximate PRC only after estimating a defensible annual spatial surface correction independently. The 2014–2024 endpoint PRC map/ranks are unaffected by corrections confined to 2021.


## Calibration hypothesis — positive snow/albedo amplification curve, rather than year-on-year tuning

The investigator requested calibration of the **surface snow / visible albedo → VIIRS radiance gain** from the existing observations, with the critical physical constraint that removal of a positive 2021 snow-reflectance artefact **cannot increase the corrected 2021 radiance**. Correcting 2021 to make its temporal trajectory plausible remains an independent post-fit quality test, not a chosen correction amplitude or reference to interpolate.

Stage \`src/calibrate_prc_2021_snow_albedo_gain.py\`, described in \`docs/prc_2021_snow_albedo_gain_calibration.md\`, estimates monotone \`G=1+beta_s * positive_snow_anomaly_fraction + beta_a * positive_visible_albedo_anomaly_fraction\` with beta coefficients constrained >=0. Candidate \`R_2021_corrected = R_2021_observed/G\` is nonnegative and never exceeds observed radiance, without arbitrary pixel clipping or a regional factor chosen to match adjacent years. 2021–flanking-year matched-month radiance differences remain the training response with independent intercept, February, cloud-free and baseline-radiance terms; this is an **observational, not fully causally identified** surface-gain model.

Pre-specify snow-only, albedo-only and combined fits to the *same valid matched coarse MOD10CM cell × brightness groups*, robust regression with a region-month balanced objective, leave-whole-NUTS3-out validation, March placebo, and no annual 2021 modifications. Interpretation must address snow/albedo collinearity, daylight broadband albedo versus actual DNB wavelength response, and physically plausible fitted gains. The full 18-region original matched group table exists on EcoVis, not as an uploaded repository source. Run the synthetic self-test followed by the full stage and review fitted coefficients + holdout diagnostics, then decide on spatial and temporal transfer. The annual -0.235 correction remains separate and unchanged.


## Results: constrained snow/albedo amplification calibration

The investigator supplied \`surface_gain_summary.txt\`, \`surface_gain_calibration_coefficients.csv\` and \`surface_gain_holdout_diagnostics.csv\` from \`src/calibrate_prc_2021_snow_albedo_gain.py\`, which operated on **8,805** matched snow-cell × brightness-class × month groups in **18 NUTS3 regions**, 5,760 in Jan–Feb fitting. January and February used matched 2020, 2021 and 2022 monthly EOG VIIRS data with MOD10CM snow and VNP43MA3 albedo; March held out as a seasonal control. The 2017+ annual EOG baseline adjustment was **not changed** or reapplied.

**Estimated monotone amplification relationships**, where increases are FRACTIONS and correction is \`R2021/G\`, \`G>=1\`:

- Snow-only \`G = 1 + 2.451746 Δsnow+\`
- Albedo-only \`G = 1 + 6.400232 Δalbedo+\`
- Combined \`G = 1 + 1.276811 Δsnow+ + 4.628849 Δalbedo+\`

**Out-of-region validation** (RMSE in nW cm^-2 sr^-1; all three fits use common QA-complete group cohort):

| Model | Jan RMSE before → after | Feb before → after | Mar control before → after | Regions improved Jan/Feb/Mar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Snow only | 15.1603 → 4.2469 (72.0%) | 24.7810 → 5.5807 (77.5%) | 3.8044 → 3.3798 (11.2%) | 15/18, 13/18, 7/18 |
| Albedo only | 15.1603 → 3.0218 (80.1%) | 24.7810 → 6.2463 (74.8%) | 3.8044 → 2.9726 (21.9%) | 14/18, 13/18, 6/18 |
| Combined | 15.1603 → 3.1191 (79.4%) | 24.7810 → 4.2441 (82.9%) | 3.8044 → 2.8334 (25.5%) | 15/18, 13/18, 6/18 |

Note the combined model is **not the best January model**; the albedo-only model has slightly lower heldout Jan RMSE. March is not a completely null negative control: combined correction reduces aggregate error ~25.5% but improves only 6/18 regional residuals. The small March median removed radiance (0.0034 nW) suggests high-leverage observations deserve scrutiny.

**Magnitude warnings:** 34.2% of Jan groups and 28.6% of Feb groups have \`G>2\` under the combined model (corresponding to >50% radiance reductions). Maximum held-out gains are 4.61 (Jan) and 4.54 (Feb), i.e. ~78% correction at the extreme, without an independent physical test of the estimated amplification. Example for the combined curve: +10 percentage points visible albedo alone produces \`G=1.4629\`, nominal 31.6% removal; +50 points snow cover alone gives \`G=1.6384\`, 39.0% removal; both together give \`G=2.1013\`, 52.4% removal. Such responses can be physically possible under snow but must be independently validated. Snow and albedo are correlated (joint snow coefficient 1.277 vs 2.452 snow only, albedo 4.629 vs 6.400 albedo only); coefficient stability remains unassessed.

**Important model-form caveat:** the training equation is an **additive anomaly regression** with covariates and a baseline-radiance×surface-contrast term; the operational correction \`Robserved / G\` is a separate multiplicative assumption. With nonzero nuisance terms they are not exactly algebraically equivalent. Reconcile training and correction forms explicitly before calling this a calibrated physical amplification law.

**Interpretation/next action:** These are preliminary *observational* snow/albedo-associated amplifications; they are not a validated causal snow-reflectance law, or a snow-free counterfactual for annual VIIRS. Review full held-out group and NUTS/month records, effect by brightness strata, slopes and uncertainty across held-out NUTS regions, placebo/control months and snow-free stable areas, and whether gain clipping/denominator effects distort dark pixels. A normal-looking 2021 annual PRC trajectory will be a final validation, **not** a coefficient selection goal. Translating monthly calibrated contrasts into annual EOG VNL composites must account for actual cloud-free sampling/annual compositing, not assume uniform months. Distinguish a standardized artificial-emission baseline from real snow-mediated night-sky visibility experienced by nocturnal animals; they are different quantities. No original annual 2014–2024 PRC data has been overwritten or approved for a 2021 correction.


## Direct multiplicative surface-gain re-fit staged (not yet evaluated)

Following the earlier empirical 18-region constrained 2021 snow/albedo experiment, the investigator requested direct multiplicative fitting rather than the earlier *additive anomaly* regression followed by post-hoc \`R_observed/G\` correction.

Added \`src/fit_prc_2021_multiplicative_gain.py\`: directly minimize observed Jan/Feb 2021 monthly VIIRS radiance residuals under \`R21 = B × T × G + error\`, with \`B=(R2020+R2022)/2\` for matched month/location, \`T=exp(γ0+γFeb*I(Feb)+γCF*delta_cloudfree/10+γbrightness*log1p(B)/log(11))\` as unconstrained background/nuisance multiplicative variation, and \`G=1+βsnow*snow_increase_fraction+βalbedo*visible_albedo_increase_fraction\`, β>=0. Compare background-only, snow-only, albedo-only and snow+albedo on the same QA-complete group cohort; exclude baseline \`B<0.2\` where gain is weakly identifiable. Use predeclared robust residual loss and region-month balanced weights, **leave whole NUTS3 regions out**, hold March out of all fitting, and calculate both direct VIIRS prediction error and retrospective-to-flanks residual (the latter must not be used to choose fitted coefficients). Operational snow-associated correction is \`R21/G\`, exactly reusing the model-estimated G and never increasing radiance. Quantify region-held-out coefficient ranges, brightness stratum results, amplifications >2 and regions improving/worsening.

Documentation \`docs/prc_2021_direct_multiplicative_test.md\`. First \`--self-test\`, then full 18-region run; reports in \`data/processed/historical_prc_2021_multiplicative_calibration/\`. The new cohort is truncated below 0.2 nW and optimization changed so previous additive summary RMSE figures are **not strictly paired**. This stage does **not** overwrite any annual radiance/PRC layers, estimate sky-polarization changes directly, or establish a snow-free emission baseline. Gain/background factor identifiability and albedo-snow collinearity remain open caveats.


## Results of the direct multiplicative 2021 snow/albedo calibration

Received and reviewed the complete requested \`multiplicative_summary.txt\`, \`multiplicative_holdout_diagnostics.csv\`, \`multiplicative_coefficient_stability.csv\`, and \`multiplicative_brightness_strata.csv\`, generated by \`src/fit_prc_2021_multiplicative_gain.py\`. Inputs represent **8,805** MOD10CM coarse snow-cell × baseline brightness classes × months, **18 NUTS3 regions**, and **5,760** winter training groups. Model optimized \`R21 = B*T*G\` directly using January/February matched calendar-month 2020/22 EOG radiance as B; nuisance T includes month, cloud-free and baseline-brightness terms; corrected radiance \`R21/G\` uses exactly the fitted G, never increases R.

Full 18-region fitted snow/albedo amplification coefficients:
- Snow only: \`beta_snow=2.511933\`; albedo only: \`beta_albedo=6.160761\`;
- Combined: \`beta_snow=1.626479\`, \`beta_albedo=3.788066\`: \`G=1+1.626479*positive_delta_snow_fraction+3.788066*positive_delta_visible_albedo_fraction\`.
- Background-only comparator explicitly estimated, with \`G=1\`.

**Independent NUTS3-held-out 2021 observed-radiance prediction RMSE (nW cm^-2 sr^-1):**

| Month | Background only | Snow only | Albedo only | Combined |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Jan | 8.9022 | 7.8958 | **5.1949** | 5.5199 |
| Feb | 12.6559 | 10.2257 | 10.2941 | **8.7689** |
| Mar (unseen-month control) | 13.2994 | 4.4530 | **3.9440** | 4.3761 |

The combined model improves observed-R21 RMSE versus nuisance-only by **38.0% Jan**, **30.7% Feb**; albedo-only is slightly better in January. March strongly improves in the surface models too, so **the background-only nuisance factor extrapolates poorly to March and March is not a decisive negative control** for this full prediction equation. When analyzing only \`R21/G\` vs the 2020/22 B proxy, combined RMSE is \`15.1603→3.0301\` Jan, \`24.7810→4.0869\` Feb, \`3.8044→2.9707\` Mar; only 6/18 March regional averages improve. Using these flank residuals as proof of an independent surface calibration would be circular, because B was used in the trained observation model.

**Geographical coefficient stability, 18 leave-one-NUTS3-out refits:** the combined \`beta_snow\` median 1.6274, p10–p90 1.5322–1.7607, min–max 1.4324–1.8856; combined \`beta_albedo\` median 3.7691, p10–p90 3.4403–3.9393, min–max 3.1511–4.2176. Coefficients do not collapse to zero nor hit fitted max 15; this is promising internal robustness but 18 overlapping leave-one-out training sets are **not confidence intervals** or independent physical replicates. Snow and albedo correlate and conditional individual coefficients remain causally uncertain.

**Brightness strata under combined model:** January 0.2–1 nW baseline mean \`G=1.8451\`, mean radiance removed \`0.3744\` nW, flank RMSE \`0.4725→0.2018\`; February for 0.2–1 mean \`G=1.4936\`, mean removed \`0.3327\` nW, flank RMSE \`0.6774→0.2446\`. For 1–5 nW: Jan mean \`G=2.0040\` / removed \`1.9008\`, Feb \`G=1.6063\` / removed \`1.5967\`. For >20 nW Jan mean \`G=1.8736\`, mean removed 20.74; Feb mean \`G=1.5803\`, mean removed 25.73. Thus large brightness corrections extend to relatively dim sites where the PRC function is most responsive; this matters biologically. **The analysis excluded baseline <0.2 nW**, and we cannot extrapolate to the darkest habitats without further controls.

**Amplification magnitude:** in held-out combined fits \`G>2\` for **33.75% Jan** and **28.31% Feb** groups; maximum gains ~4.565 Jan and 4.397 Feb. Relative to the candidate correction this is >50% subtraction for many groups. **Do not equate plausible sign/stability with validated physical amplitudes.**

**Current research decision:** the direct multiplicative model removes the previous additive-fit/multiplicative-application mismatch and has stable internal holdout coefficients; **it has not yet earned adoption for annual VIIRS or PRC**. Critical next validation: snow-free matched pixels and NUTS regions, warm-month 2021 contrasts (the full monthly EOG panel exists), held-out region-level trend checks separated from fitting, sensitivity to 2022 VIIRS platform/product changes, and explicit check whether T absorbs genuine lighting changes. Also assess alternate B reference years when available, physically plausible gain bounds, and spatial uncertainty; only then apply cloudfree-weighted annual correction using actual annual composite specification. Historical 2014–2024 endpoints and ranks unchanged. No dataset overwritten.

# Behavioural thresholds for polarization vision in arthropods: a historical and comparative literature review

**Research literature note — 6 November 2026**  
**Project:** European artificial light at night, polarization receptor contrast, and celestial navigation (2014–2024)  
**Status:** Extended research synthesis for the lab notebook; **not** a formally exhaustive systematic review or a validated set of spider-specific psychometric thresholds.  
**Scope:** Early literature (including 1924 and work from the 1940s–1960s) to November 2026, emphasizing insects, spiders, and other arthropods.

## 1. Executive summary

The reviewed literature does **not** support a universal degree-of-polarization (DoP; more precisely, for linearly polarized stimuli, DoLP) threshold for arthropod behaviour. It supports a wide range of task- and species-specific limits. Some of the strongest numerical results are:

| Taxon/species | Behavioural DoP finding | Task | Status/qualification |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Field cricket, *Gryllus campestris* | **5–7%** threshold; some individual responses at 3% | Celestial e-vector orientation | Direct graded-DoP behavioural experiment [Henze & Labhart 2007](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.007831) |
| Honeybee, *Apis mellifera* | Roughly **7–15%** transition, often summarized as ~10%; orientation demonstrated at 10% | Skylight compass and dance-related orientation | Historical estimates and a demonstrated stimulus, **not** a universal lower bound; von Frisch; [Edrich & von Helversen 1987](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00317983) |
| Nocturnal dung beetle, *Escarabaeus satyrus* (also printed as *Scarabaeus satyrus*) | Behavioural threshold constrained between **4–32%**, possible use at 11% | Nocturnal celestial compass | Experimental resolution limited; **0.23 is not the demonstrated threshold** [Foster et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.188532) |
| *Daphnia magna* | Approximately **16%** | Swimming orientation to overhead polarized illumination | Lowest supporting DoP reported [Novales Flamarique & Browman 2000](https://doi.org/10.1007/s003590000162) |
| *Daphnia pulex* | Approximately **31%** | As above | Related species, different threshold; same paper |
| Copepod *Pontella karachiensis* | **20–30%** interval; significant at 30%, not at 23–24% | Polarotaxis | Depends on irradiance [Manor et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2009.07.007) |
| Springtail *Podura aquatica* | **10.1–25.5%** | Attraction to horizontally polarized water-like reflection | Polarotaxis, **not** skylight compass [Egri et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.139295) |
| Damselflies, mayflies, horseflies | Approx. **>0–92%** across the tested taxa, depending strongly on species and wavelength | Polarized water-surface attraction | Threshold intervals in controlled choice/field experiments [Kriska et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinsphys.2009.08.013) |
| Hermit crabs (*Coenobita* spp.; *Pagurus bernhardus*) | **17–22%** fitted half-maximal responses | Polarization-contrast detection of predator-like looming stimuli | A contrast-response parameter, **not** a minimum skylight DoP |
| Ground spider *Drassodes cupreus* and other spiders | **No validated numerical minimum** | Celestial orientation, homing, or discrimination | Polarization use established; graded behavioural threshold remains unresolved |

**Central conclusion:** A numerical threshold obtained from a bee, cricket, dung beetle, water insect, or crustacean should **not** be assigned to *Drassodes* or *Helicoverpa* as if it were a measured species-specific limit. It can, however, be used as an explicitly labelled **cross-taxon sensitivity benchmark**.

**Source caution:** References below collect the studies identified during the November 2026 literature search. Bibliographic details and original threshold definitions should be checked against original full-text methods before manuscript submission. Not every older source was available as a fully searchable primary paper; references marked as contextual may require follow-up verification.

## 2. Distinguishing different kinds of thresholds

1. **Behavioural absolute DoP threshold:** lowest externally presented DoP yielding statistically demonstrable orientation, attraction, or discrimination under specified lighting and geometry.
2. **Behavioural transition or half-maximal DoP:** fit-derived midpoint or interval across a psychometric response curve; **not equivalent** to the lowest demonstrable DoP.
3. **Polarization-*contrast* threshold:** difference in polarization state between object and background detectable by the animal. It need not equal an absolute DoP threshold for an extended celestial pattern.
4. **Neural threshold:** lowest degree of polarization eliciting a reliable photoreceptor or interneuron response; this is **not** direct evidence of successful behaviour.
5. **Absolute irradiance threshold:** minimum light flux for an orientation response at a given polarization; despite the name, it is **not** a minimum DoP.
6. **Polarization sensitivity (PS):** usually a ratio of receptor responses for different e-vector angles; **PS = 9 does not mean DoP = 9%**.

The distinction between a **detected** stimulus and one yielding **accurate, reliable navigation** is fundamental. A reduced concentration of headings at low DoP may indicate degraded compass precision without complete loss of cue use.

## 3. Celestial compass studies with relevant quantitative evidence

### 3.1 Honeybees

Von Frisch's classic honeybee work introduced the functional skylight-polarization compass; later accounts describe an approximately **7–15%** transition from reliably oriented to poorly oriented behaviour (often summarized as ~10%). In their 2007 cricket comparison, Henze & Labhart discuss this historical honeybee benchmark.

[Edrich & von Helversen (1987)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00317983) demonstrated that bees could orient to artificial zenithal polarization at **10%** DoP, but **did not test lower DoP**. Therefore “usable at 10%” is stronger than “requires at least 10%.”

Additional context: [von Frisch (1949)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02174424); von Frisch (1965/1967); [Rossel & Wehner (1986)](https://doi.org/10.1038/323128a0).

### 3.2 Crickets

[Brunner & Labhart (1987)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-3032.1987.tb00718.x) established behavioural e-vector orientation in crickets. [Herzmann & Labhart (1989)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00619350), despite the title *Spectral sensitivity and absolute threshold of polarization vision in crickets: a behavioral study*, primarily measured **irradiance thresholds**, not a graded minimum DoP.

**The most useful direct low-DoP experiment** is [Henze & Labhart (2007), *Journal of Experimental Biology*](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.007831). *Gryllus campestris* showed behavioural e-vector orientation near a **5–7%** threshold, with some individuals responding around **3%**. The result is an important demonstration that a celestial polarization compass may remain functional at far below 23% DoP.

### 3.3 Nocturnal dung beetles

[Dacke et al. (2003), *Nature*](https://doi.org/10.1038/424033a) demonstrated orientation using polarized moonlight; [Dacke et al. (2003), *JEB*](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.00289) treated twilight polarization in *Scarabaeus zambesianus*. Follow-up experiments included lunar orientation and navigation under different illumination regimes ([Dacke et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2003.2594); [Dacke et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2010.0191)).

[Foster et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.188532) used several graded DoP treatments (approximately **4%, 11%, 32%, and 99%**, with controls) for the nocturnal dung beetle *Escarabaeus satyrus*. Reliable orientation was demonstrated at **32%**; evidence around **11%** was suggestive/inconclusive; at **4%** behaviour was not distinguishable from unpolarized control. Thus the true minimum is unresolved within a **4–32%** interval.

**DoP 0.23 was associated with a natural lunar-sky condition/benchmark in this paper, not an experimentally demonstrated universal or spider-specific behavioural threshold.** Orientation at weak lunar polarization can be degraded yet remain nonrandom; “less reliable” should not be rewritten as “cue undetectable.”

### 3.4 Ants, locusts, and other navigators

[Vowles (1950)](https://doi.org/10.1038/165282a0) reported ant sensitivity to polarized light; subsequent *Cataglyphis* and related ant studies established skylight compass use and path integration. The reviewed material did **not** identify an equally rigorous ant-specific minimum DoP comparable to the cricket study.

[Mappes & Homberg (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00359-003-0473-4) and later locust work demonstrated functional polarization-dependent orientation in tethered flight. The locust neural studies in §6 should **not** be presented as direct behavioural thresholds.

## 4. Polarotaxis and water-surface attraction: quantitative insect and hexapod experiments

[Kriska, Bernáth, Farkas & Horváth (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinsphys.2009.08.013) compared response to polarized reflections in several water-associated insects. The following intervals are reported for the **blue band around 450 nm**; they are ranges constraining a behavioural threshold, **not precise single-valued thresholds**.

| Species | Estimated threshold DoP interval in blue light | Behaviour |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| *Enallagma cyathigerum* (damselfly) | >0 to ≤17% | Water-site attraction |
| *Ischnura elegans* (damselfly) | 17–24% | Water-site attraction |
| *Baetis rhodani* (mayfly) | 32–55% | Water-site attraction |
| *Ephemera danica* (mayfly) | 55–92% | Water-site attraction |
| *Epeorus silvicola* (mayfly) | 55–92% | Water-site attraction |
| *Rhithrogena semicolorata* (mayfly) | 55–92% | Water-site attraction |
| *Tabanus bovinus* (horsefly) | 32–55% | Polarotactic attraction |
| *Tabanus tergestinus* (horsefly) | 32–55% | Polarotactic attraction |
| *Tabanus maculicornis* (horsefly) | 55–92% | Polarotactic attraction |

**Wavelength matters substantially.** For *Ischnura elegans* the study estimated **6.8–11.0% in red**, **7.3–11.1% in green**, and **17.0–23.5% in blue**. The authors also noted incomplete knowledge of the species' spectral polarization-detection mechanisms. Do not generalize a blue-band water-reflection threshold to UV-sensitive celestial compass detection.

[Egri et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.139295) estimated **10.1–25.5% DoP** for attraction of the water-surface springtail *Podura aquatica* to horizontally polarized stimuli. Both strength and e-vector orientation matter. This is useful cross-hexapod evidence, but the task differs from compass orientation.

Additional water/host-detection context: [Schwind (1984)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00605390), [Schwind (1991)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00193544), [Kriska et al. (1998)](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.201.15.2273), Egri et al. (2012), and later horsefly and stable-fly studies.

## 5. Spiders: established polarization vision but no validated behavioural DoP threshold

### 5.1 Historical studies

| Period | Species/groups | What was demonstrated | Minimum usable DoP? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1955–1959 | *Arctosa perita*, *A. cinerea*, related wolf spiders | Papi's astronomical and polarized-skylight orientation experiments | No numerical threshold found |
| 1958–1966 | *Agelena labyrinthica* | Görner's polarized-light orientation; headings affected by rotation of overhead e-vector | No |
| 1960s | *Arctosa* and other wolf spiders | Eye-manipulation/electrophysiological evidence for eye-specific compass functions | No |
| 1966 | *Aphonopelma californicum* (tarantula) | Learned discrimination of polarized stimuli in a maze | No |
| 1999 | *Lycosa tarentula* | Anterior median eyes implicated through behaviour/physiology | No |
| 1999 | *Drassodes cupreus* | Specialized polarization-sensitive posterior median-eye optics | No |
| 2001 | *Pardosa tristis* and other spider models | Responses to rotating e-vectors with intensity controls | No |
| 2023 | *Drassodes* sp. | Several stimulus DoP levels tested | Attempted, inconclusive |

Historically useful primary sources include [Görner (1958)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00345583), [Görner (1962)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00302327), [Henton & Crawford (1966)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00343655), and work by Papi, Magni and colleagues. The original Papi and Magni bibliographic records should be checked carefully before formal citation.

### 5.2 *Drassodes cupreus*

[Dacke et al. (1999), *Nature*, “Built-in polarizers form part of a compass organ in spiders”](https://doi.org/10.1038/46773), documents specialized optics in the posterior median eyes of *Drassodes cupreus*. [Dacke, Doan & O'Carroll (2001), “Polarized light detection in spiders”](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.204.14.2481), extends the discussion of polarization-detection pathways and associated behaviour.

These papers support **polarization sensitivity and celestial orientation**, **not** a quantitative psychophysical threshold such as DoP = 0.23.

[Meah (2023), *Light pollution and navigation in two nocturnal arthropod taxa* (doctoral thesis, University of Bristol)](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/studentTheses/light-pollution-and-navigation-in-two-nocturnal-arthropod-taxa/) is particularly relevant. Tests with *Drassodes* used stimulus DoP values around **0.37, 0.63, 0.74 and 0.99**. Turning and orientation results were inconsistent and affected by directional biases, preventing a reliable fit of a behavioural threshold. These treatments do **not** establish that 37% is a required minimum: failure to resolve a dose–response is not evidence of a detection limit.

### 5.3 Wolf spiders and tarantulas

The Papi/Görner historical studies and later *Lycosa* and *Pardosa* research broaden polarization vision beyond the unusual optical structure of *Drassodes*. [Henton & Crawford (1966)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00343655) demonstrated trained polarization discrimination in a tarantula, *Aphonopelma californicum*; the trained maze response is a different task from spontaneous homing.

**Spider conclusion:** No reviewed peer-reviewed source gives a robust minimum DoP for successful celestial navigation in *Drassodes*, *Arctosa*, *Pardosa*, *Lycosa*, *Agelena*, or *Aphonopelma*. A species-specific numerical cutoff remains an important experimental gap.

## 6. Other crustaceans: absolute orientation, polarization contrast, and neural limits

### 6.1 Direct orientation thresholds

[Novales Flamarique & Browman (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s003590000162) compared graded wavelength-dependent polarization orientation in two *Daphnia* species:

| Species | Approximate lowest supporting DoP | Qualification |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| *Daphnia magna* | **16%** | Directional swimming to overhead polarization |
| *Daphnia pulex* | **31%** | Same broad task; different spectral sensitivity |

Older cladoceran studies include Baylor & Smith (1953), Baylor & Hazen (1962), and Hazen & Baylor (1962); secondary reviews sometimes repeat **10–20%** for *Daphnia* in general, but this should not replace the species-specific directly tested results. A related comparison reported attraction of *D. pulex* to 37% but not 22% polarized light. Historical estimates should be tracked to their original tests.

[Manor et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2009.07.007) tested the copepod *Pontella karachiensis* at calibrated DoPs around **23, 24, 30, 40, 54, 63, 73, 85 and 94%** (400–600 nm measurements). Orienting polarotaxis was significant at **30% and above**, not at 23–24%, giving an approximate **20–30%** interval. Irradiance substantially affected behaviour.

Early marine-crustacean orientation studies by [Bainbridge & Waterman (1957)](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.34.3.342) and [Bainbridge & Waterman (1958)](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.35.3.487) included mysids and aquatic visibility/turbidity; they do not provide a directly transferable minimum DoP for nocturnal insects.

### 6.2 Polarization contrast and other tasks

| Taxon | Finding | Why it is not a celestial DoP threshold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crayfish, *Procambarus clarkii* | Dorsal light-reflex response near DoP **13%** under a specified ~20° e-vector contrast | Depends on angular stimulus contrast and reflex |
| Fiddler crab, *Uca vomeris* | Detection at around DoP **8%** under defined contrast conditions | Object/background discrimination |
| Mantis shrimp, *Haptosquilla trispinosa* | Approximately **20 percentage points** of polarization contrast in one discrimination task | Threshold on *difference*, not absolute background DoP |
| Hermit crabs, *Coenobita rugosus*, *C. brevimanus*, *Pagurus bernhardus* | Fitted half-maximal responses approximately **17–22%** | Predator-like looming stimulus against a polarized background (50% in the experiment) |
| Five crustacean species in gaze-stabilization tests | Polarization contrast alone did not reliably stabilize gaze | Negative result for *this task*, not proof of absent polarization perception |

Sources include [Glantz & Schroeter (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00359-006-0191-9), [How et al. (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.103457), [Smithers, Roberts & How (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aax3572), [Drerup & How (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.229898), and How et al. (2023) on hermit crabs ([publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00359-023-01631-z)).

## 7. Neural thresholds and why they should not replace behaviour

| Taxon | Study | Approximate neural DoP finding | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Gryllus campestris* | [Labhart (1996)](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.199.7.1467) | e-vector information retained near **0.05** | Consistent with, but distinct from, later behavioural threshold |
| *Schistocerca gregaria* | [Pfeiffer et al. (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00480.2010) | Some anterior-optic-tubercle neuronal responses near **0.30** | Particular neuron class and experimental conditions |
| *Schistocerca gregaria* | [Hensgen et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00359-022-01545-2) | Some central-complex coding near **0.05** | Different stages of neural circuit; not necessarily contradiction |
| *Gryllus bimaculatus* | Sakura and colleagues (2007/2008) | Coding assessed across approx. **0.18–0.99** | Lowest *tested* is not automatically the functional threshold |
| *Drassodes cupreus* | [Dacke et al. (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1038/46773) and [Dacke et al. (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.204.14.2481) | Strongly polarization-sensitive eye structures | No measured minimum for homing |

Photoreceptor sensitivity, pooled neural reliability, absolute photon catch and decision threshold are all different quantities. A compass based on broad sky integration may operate at much lower DoP than a rapid, spatially localized predator detector. The same animal may succeed at one polarization task but fail another.

## 11. Consequences for the European light-pollution PRC maps

### 11.1 DoLP is **not** automatically receptor contrast

For linearly polarized illumination the physical stimulus can be described by

```text
DoLP = sqrt(Q^2 + U^2) / I
```

The project's spider data are **polarization receptor contrast (PRC)** obtained from the locked empirical VIIRS→PRC transfer and not a direct measurement of the sky's DoLP. A numerical **DoLP = 0.23** must not be interpreted as **PRC = 0.23**.

This project already defines an **illustrative**, model-dependent mapping with orthogonal receptors, optimally aligned e-vector and receptor polarization sensitivity **Sp = 10**:

```text
PRC = ln(R_parallel / R_perp)
PRC_ref(DoLP, Sp) = 2 * atanh(DoLP * (Sp - 1) / (Sp + 1))
PRC_ref(DoLP = 0.23, Sp = 10) = 0.3809031327477229
```

See [the existing threshold scenario methods note](../annual_nuts3_spider_prc_dop023_threshold.md). **0.3809 PRC is a scenario cutoff under these assumptions, NOT an experimentally validated *Drassodes* behavioural threshold.**

### 11.2 Recommended sensitivity analysis

For context and visual comparison, explore a **DoLP** benchmark series of 0.05, 0.10, 0.16, 0.23 and 0.32:

| Reference DoLP | Origin and appropriate label |
| ---: | --- |
| 0.05 | Very weak celestial polarization; near cricket behavioural/neural limit |
| 0.10 | Approximate historical honeybee benchmark |
| 0.16 | Approximate *Daphnia magna* orientation result (different task) |
| 0.23 | Nocturnal natural-sky comparison in dung-beetle work |
| 0.32 | Dung-beetle stimulus supporting clear orientation |

These values are **references across different taxa and tasks**, not a *Drassodes* threshold series. If comparing to **PRC maps**, convert each DoLP benchmark with the explicitly chosen receptor model and preserve separate labels/units; do not threshold PRC by the raw DoLP value.

The current project threshold maps classify **NUTS3 mean PRC** and area-weight **whole regions** using fixed valid-pixel-supported 2014 areas. That statistic is **not** the physical area of all individual VIIRS pixels below threshold. A true pixel-level low-PRC area estimate requires classification **before** NUTS aggregation. See [threshold map production methods](../annual_nuts3_spider_prc_dop023_threshold.md).

A defensible manuscript approach is to plot time series of European area/region-share falling below several labelled benchmarks, alongside changes in the continuous PRC distributions and model sensitivity to Sp. This demonstrates whether historical trends are robust to uncertain biological cutoffs without asserting a false universal threshold.

### 11.3 Experiment needed for *Drassodes*

A future graded-DoP orientation experiment could sample, for example, 0, 0.03, 0.05, 0.07, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.23, 0.30, 0.40, 0.60 and 0.90, with photon flux, spectrum and e-vector orientation controlled separately. Measure circular concentration of heading, homing error and fraction of oriented trials, rather than only turning. Fit a psychometric relationship to estimate (i) lowest reliably detected cue, (ii) half-maximal performance, and (iii) near-optimal compass performance. Test irradiance dependence under nocturnal conditions.

## 12. Conclusions and limitations

1. **Strongest direct low-DoP celestial threshold:** field cricket around **5–7%**; a functional compass can use very weak polarization.
2. **Most directly relevant nocturnal comparison:** dung beetle minimum is bounded only broadly between **4% and 32%**. **0.23 should not be called a hard threshold.**
3. **Ecological task changes the number:** water-attraction, swimming orientation, object detection and celestial compass performance are not interchangeable.
4. **Spiders remain uncalibrated behaviourally:** specialized *Drassodes* polarization detection is well documented, but the minimum DoP sustaining reliable homing has **not** been established in the reviewed studies.
5. **PRC is not DoLP:** the project's illustrative conversion of DoLP 0.23 gives **PRC ~0.3809 for Sp = 10** under specified assumptions; this is neither an animal-level performance limit nor a receptor-independent constant.

**Outstanding verification before publication:** Check the full texts and bibliographic details of historical spider papers, threshold calibration procedures for all quantitative studies, confidence intervals and statistical criteria for “threshold” versus “minimum tested usable level,” any as-yet-unindexed recent arthropod papers, and the spectral/irradiance context of each experiment. This file records the literature search's evidence and uncertainties, rather than silently converting disparate experiments into one universal number.

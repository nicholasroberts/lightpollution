# Before/after radiance corrections — talk publication figures

**Prepared 4 November 2026, branch `publication-figures`. EcoVis render and scientific QA pending.**

## What this is designed to communicate

The analysis uncovered two different artefacts/anomalies that would distort a naive 2014–2024 artificial light at night trajectory:

1. **Early-2017 series step** in the annual VIIRS product: the selected **0.235 nW cm⁻² sr⁻¹ additive positive-pixel offset** starts on 2017 data, with zero clipped at zero, calibrated before NUTS3 means are extracted. This is **not** a post hoc 0.235 subtraction from NUTS3 means. Archived raw and calibrated NUTS3 columns both exist in the identical NUTS2016 annual parquet; this script uses those *actual* columns, not a synthetic reverse transformation.
2. **2021 anomalous winter albedo/snow response**: an **independently locked native-pixel annual VNL raster overlay**, using the accepted snow/albedo model, revises only 2021 original pixels and applies the existing 2017+ zero offset once afterwards. It preserves zero and valid pixel masks and never increases radiance. The source is model `viirs_annual_2021_snow_albedo_2026_10_20`, locked `config/viirs_2021_snow_correction.yaml`. This adjustment may not remove the full 2021 pulse and cannot be treated as proof that every remaining fluctuation is real.

No parameters are re-estimated and all scientific source data remain untouched.

## Talk-method summary (source-grounded, not a manuscript claim)

### 2017 baseline (roughly 140 words)

The annual VIIRS Day/Night Band radiance series showed an abrupt change around 2017 that could not safely be interpreted as a genuine, continent-wide change in artificial lighting. The project examined the discontinuity using fixed NUTS2016 regions, direct comparisons of persistently illuminated pixels, and independent brightness-stratified and segmented historical time-series tests. Those diagnostics supported an approximately additive offset of 0.235 nW cm⁻² sr⁻¹ in the post-2016 data. The locked correction is consequently applied to each positive native VIIRS pixel from 2017 onward, subtracting 0.235 and setting any negative result to zero; original zero/background pixels remain zero. Only then are area-weighted NUTS3 means calculated. This distinction matters: subtracting a constant directly from each regional mean would not reproduce the actual pixel-level adjustment, especially in dark regions. The corrected series can now be compared across the 2016–2017 transition without carrying forward the identified measurement offset, although it retains genuine variations and other measurement uncertainties.

### 2021 snow/albedo (roughly 155 words)

The 2021 annual VIIRS radiance series exhibited an anomalous brightening that was examined using monthly observations, snow cover and visible surface albedo. In particular, winter comparisons with the same months in 2020 and 2022 helped identify a geographically uneven January–February excess, rather than assuming that the annual increase represented a sudden change in artificial lighting. The approved correction uses the previously tested spatial snow/albedo model to estimate the anomalous winter contribution at the native annual VIIRS pixel scale. A locked European raster patch modifies the affected 2021 raw annual pixels, preserving original zero and missing-data masks, and is constrained not to brighten pixels. The standard post-2016 0.235 nW cm⁻² sr⁻¹ baseline correction is then applied once to positive pixels before recalculating NUTS3 radiance and receptor contrast. The model is not a simple subtraction from annual regional means and does not independently reconstruct corrected monthly imagery. Because the annual VIIRS composite cannot be exactly reversed from monthly products, some residual 2021 anomaly may remain.
